The following describes the design for a local queue management system utilizing Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) on the Android platform, incorporating the required use of a Foreground Service for client reliability.

I. Queue Server (Peripheral) Operations

The device hosting the queue (the Server) operates continuously in the foreground as a BLE Peripheral.

    Queue Initialization: The server application collects the queue details (e.g., @queuename, waittime, currentnumber) and formats this data into a packet that adheres to the 31-byte BLE Advertisement payload limit.

    Continuous Broadcasting: The Peripheral initiates a continuous, high-frequency non-connectable advertising regimen. This broadcast constantly and repeatedly transmits the formatted queue status packet, ensuring high reliability and rapid discovery by nearby devices.

    Foreground Assumption: By operating in the foreground, the Peripheral bypasses all Android power management restrictions that would otherwise throttle or halt background advertising.

II. Queue Client (Central) Operations

The user device joining the queue (the Client) operates in the Central role, with its scanner logic running within a dedicated Foreground Service.

    Discovery (Foreground): The client application runs a standard BLE scan, receiving the continuous broadcasts from nearby Peripherals. The received data is parsed and displayed to the user as available queues (the "radar").

    Joining and Connection: Upon user selection, the client initiates a GATT connection to the chosen Peripheral. It transmits its unique device ID (via a GATT Write operation) and receives its sequential ticket number from the Peripheral. The client then cleanly disconnects.

    Monitoring (Foreground Service): After disconnection, the client initiates a Foreground Service to manage the subsequent monitoring phase.

        Persistent Scanning: The service immediately begins a continuous BLE scan, utilizing the granted background and location permissions. This dedicated service prevents the Android OS from throttling or killing the scanning process.

        Data Filtration: The scanner efficiently filters for the specific Peripheral's advertisements.

    Alarm Trigger: When the Foreground Service receives a broadcast packet where the advertised currentnumber matches the client's assigned ticket number, the following sequence occurs:

        The service instantly executes the alarm logic.

        The BLE scan is immediately terminated to conserve power.

        The Foreground Service is stopped (dismissing its persistent notification).

        A high-priority, system-level alarm (e.g., a full-screen notification or loud audio alert) is triggered to immediately notify the user.
